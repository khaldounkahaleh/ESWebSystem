Kuwait Indemnity Calculator — Frequently Asked Questions

What is end-of-service indemnity in Kuwait?
End-of-service indemnity is a mandatory legal monetary payout that employers in the Kuwait private sector must give to employees upon the termination or resignation of their employment contract, as dictated by Kuwait Labor Law No. 6 of 2010.
How is indemnity calculated for private-sector workers?
Under Article 51, the calculation is split into two blocks:
  • For the first 5 years: 15 days of salary for each year worked.
  • For years beyond 5 years: 30 days of salary for each year worked.
The total cap cannot exceed 1.5 years (18 months) of salary.
Does the calculator use my basic salary or total salary?
Legally, the calculation must be based on your latest remuneration, which includes your basic salary plus any regular allowances (like housing or transport) stipulated in your contract. It should not be calculated on basic salary alone unless no allowances exist.
What happens to my indemnity if I resign before 3 years?
If you are on an open-ended contract and resign before completing a full 3 years of continuous service, you are not entitled to any indemnity under Kuwait law.
How much indemnity do I get if I resign between 3 to 5 years?
If you resign after 3 years but before reaching 5 years of service, you are entitled to one-third (33.3%) of your total accumulated indemnity calculation.
How much indemnity do I get if I resign between 5 to 10 years?
If you resign after completing 5 years but before reaching 10 years of service, you are entitled to two-thirds (66.6%) of your total accumulated indemnity calculation.
Do I get full indemnity if I resign after 10 years?
Yes. Once you have completed 10 or more years of continuous service, you are entitled to 100% (full) indemnity, even if you choose to resign voluntarily.
What if my employer terminates my contract? Do I get full indemnity?
Yes. If your employer terminates your contract (laid off, company restructuring, etc.), you are entitled to 100% full indemnity from year one, provided you have completed your initial probation period. The 3-year minimum rule only applies if you resign.
Is there a maximum limit (cap) on the indemnity payout?
Yes. According to Kuwait Labor Law, the total end-of-service indemnity payout cannot exceed a maximum value equivalent to 18 months (1.5 years) of your latest salary.
How does a fixed-term contract affect my indemnity calculation?
If you are on a fixed-term contract (e.g., a 2-year contract) and it expires without renewal, you are entitled to full indemnity for those years. If you break a fixed contract early by resigning, you may forfeit indemnity or be liable for damages, depending on the contract clauses.
Are part-time employees entitled to indemnity in Kuwait?
No, typical part-time workers or those on casual temporary contracts are generally not covered under standard end-of-service indemnity benefits unless explicitly written into their employment agreement.
Can an employer legally deduct money from my indemnity?
An employer can only deduct funds from your indemnity if you owe an official debt to the company (e.g., unreturned company property, salary advances) or if a court order mandates a deduction. They cannot arbitrarily reduce it.
Can I lose my indemnity entirely if I am fired?
Yes, under Article 41 of the Labor Law, you can be dismissed without indemnity for severe misconduct, such as committing a crime at work, causing intentional financial loss to the employer, or being absent without excuse for more than 7 consecutive days.
Does probation time count toward my indemnity?
Yes. If you pass your probation period and continue working for the company, the initial probation months are legally included as part of your total continuous service timeline.
What should I do if my employer miscalculates or refuses to pay my indemnity?
If there is a dispute, you should gather your employment contract, bank statements, and salary slips, and file an official complaint with the Public Authority for Manpower (PAM) or the Ministry of Social Affairs and Labor dispute department.
